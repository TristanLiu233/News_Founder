# Industry Intelligence & Ranking Platform

## 1. Project Mission

- Build a bilingual intelligence product for Computer, Finance, and Gaming.
- Preserve source-native facts and history, resolve them to canonical subjects, and expose auditable retrieval and ranking.
- Treat public-source access, provenance, stable identity, and conservative entity resolution as product requirements.
- Keep large historical datasets in the backend while presenting selective, useful Top-N views in the UI.

## 2. Product Semantics

- `Subject` is the canonical entity and the unit returned by Search and Trending.
- `RankObservation` is an append-only observation from a source that explicitly publishes a ranking.
- Ranking position represents source-native rank and may participate in composite ranking.
- A chronological news/feed position represents recency, not importance.
- Finance News Intelligence and Finance Market Pulse are separate product concepts.
- News Intelligence contains news and chronological feeds.
- Market Pulse contains crypto trends, market-cap lists, active stocks, trading-volume lists, and similar market rankings.
- News/feed items must never be fed into ranking RRF as though their feed order were an importance rank.
- A news item links to its original external article URL.
- A subject is not owned by or directly linked to an arbitrary news provider.
- One content item may relate to multiple canonical subjects.
- Search returns one canonical subject once; related content supplies context rather than becoming a duplicate subject.
- Composite Ranking measures cross-platform attention, not quality, investment merit, or prediction.
- External Company Rankings are publisher rankings shown as published: External Rankings != Our Opinion.
- Today != Trending != Search != Company Rankings. Trending RRF and Search relevance are never used as company rankings, and company rankings never feed Trending or Search.
- A `Company` (Phase 4) is a minimal cross-domain identity for external company rankings; it is separate from `Subject`. Phase 5 attaches sourced profile facts through `CompanyProfile`/`CompanyProfileSource`; a Company may be deterministically linked to a Subject, but the two are never the same record.
- Company Rankings != Company Profiles; Company Profile != AI-generated analysis. A profile shows source-published facts with provenance; it never asserts competitive advantages, scores, or investment views.
- Different company rankings (market cap, revenue, total assets) are never combined into a platform company score.
- An `Event` (Phase 6) is one concrete, time-bounded real-world occurrence reported by one or more persisted ContentItems: Subject = who/what, Event = what happened, ContentItem = which report covered it. Topics such as "AI" or "NVIDIA" are Subjects, never Events. `SubjectType.EVENT` (ranked story items) is unrelated to Phase 6 Events.
- Events are evidence-backed clusters of existing ContentItems built by deterministic rules; they are not AI-generated, scored, ranked, or predicted. `source_count` is coverage, not importance.
- Today != Trending != Search != Rankings != Companies != Events. Events never feed Trending RRF, Search, or company rankings, and Search does not return Events.
- A wrong Event merge is worse than a duplicate Event: grouping prefers false negatives.

## 3. Architecture & Tech Stack

- Backend: Python 3.12, FastAPI, SQLAlchemy 2, Alembic, PostgreSQL.
- PostgreSQL extensions: `pg_trgm` and `vector`/pgvector.
- Frontend: Next.js App Router, React, TypeScript, CSS.
- HTTP acquisition: `httpx` through the shared `HTTPClient`.
- Validation: pytest, Ruff, ESLint, TypeScript, and Next.js production build.
- Source adapters normalize external records at the boundary.
- Ranking ingestion writes append-only source snapshots and ingestion batches.
- Canonical resolution uses identities, aliases, normalization, and conservative matching.
- Search uses a materialized `SearchDocument` projection per canonical subject.
- Lexical retrieval combines exact, alias, prefix, PostgreSQL FTS, and pg_trgm candidates.
- Optional semantic retrieval uses an `EmbeddingProvider` abstraction and pgvector storage.
- Search RRF and Trending RRF are separate scoring contexts.

## 4. Permanent Design Rules

- Read this file before changing the repository.
- Inspect current code, migrations, tests, and README before relying on a historical plan.
- Preserve completed functionality and prefer incremental changes over rewrites.
- Use machine values in English: `ranking`, `feed`, `news`, `market_pulse`.
- Translate labels only in the UI; never translate source brands or source-provided titles.
- Preserve original URLs and source timestamps.
- Do not republish full copyrighted or paywalled article bodies.
- Store title, URL, publication time, source, language, legitimate short excerpt, and structured metadata.
- Prefer official API, then RSS, then public structured endpoints, then stable public HTML.
- Do not bypass login, CAPTCHA, WAF, robots restrictions, or access controls.
- Document source substitutions and their failure reasons.
- Do not modify historical Alembic revisions; add a new migration.
- Canonical Subject remains the identity source of truth.
- Entity linking must be deterministic, testable, conservative, and auditable.
- Phase 3.1 does not use LLM extraction, AI classification, summarization, or topic clustering.
- Content ↔ Subject links need exact evidence (same article, exchange code, cashtag, or a unique eligible exact name/alias; see Content ↔ Subject linking (Phase 6.1)); no link is better than a wrong link. Coverage never justifies relaxing a rule.
- Phase 6 Events are content clusters, not an Event Graph: there are no event-to-event, causal, or knowledge-graph relations.
- `EventCompanyLink` (policy DQ-3) means "this canonical Company is materially involved in this Event" (it acts, is acted on, or is a party to the transaction, ruling, or disclosure reported); it is a binary participation relation, not "primary company", not a role (acquirer/target/seller), and never "the reports mention this company". Several Companies may be linked to one Event. Accepted evidence: the deterministic chain Content → `ContentSubjectLink` → `CompanySubjectLink` (`content_subject_company`), or an explicit reviewed action (`reviewed`). Rejected as canonical evidence: raw substring, exact alias in headline or body, publisher tags/categories, product/game/brand → owner, subsidiary/brand → parent, and AI output (review suggestions only). A direct identity path (an exchange code written by the publisher, matched to an exclusive Company identity) is acceptable in principle but is not implemented while it has no live yield. A company absent from the catalog is a catalog gap, never a reason to create a Company or relax a rule. Adding role semantics needs a schema decision first.
- Keep existing market observations, snapshots, subjects, and search documents.
- New content ingestion must be idempotent.
- Historical content is retained; UI limits do not imply backend deletion.
- All timestamps are timezone-aware.
- Primary UI page-size rules:
  - Home: Top 5 highlights.
  - Today: about 5 items per source by default.
  - Trending: Top 20 by default.
  - Large datasets: View More, pagination, and filters.
- Subject Detail: current rank, source appearances (about 20 lists), rank history, and 10 related news per page.
- Do not add infinite scroll.
- Canonical Subject Detail (`/subject/{id}`) is the internal destination for Trending and Search subjects; news and source titles link to the original external URL.
- Primary navigation (since Phase 8B) is the brand (links Home `/`) plus Discover · Events · Rankings · Companies. Discover links `/today` and is active on `/today`, `/trending`, and `/search`, which share the Today | Trending | Search `DiscoverTabs` (the selected `?domain=` is kept); there is no `/discover` route. Subject, Company, and Event Detail are detail-only pages. Computer, Finance, and Gaming are tabs or filters, never top-level navigation.
- Never create a cross-domain global ranking or compare composite scores across domains.
- Page loads read persisted data only; they never trigger ingestion, ranking, reindexing, embeddings, or LLM calls.
- Frontend source filters use machine source keys from `/api/v1/sources`, never hard-coded display names.
- English and Simplified Chinese UI must remain available.
- Locale persistence must remain functional.
- Source failures must remain isolated during bulk ingestion.
- Company ranking snapshots are immutable and idempotent: a repeated `snapshot_key` is a replay and identical neighbouring content is not duplicated; history is never rewritten to simulate a latest list.
- Store the publisher's real Top N (or a documented tracked prefix); never pad or fabricate positions, metrics, or identity matches.
- Company resolution is conservative: exact identity, reviewed identity-to-identity seed pairs, unique exact name; no fuzzy matching. Exclusive identity schemes are the CompaniesMarketCap ticker symbol, SEC CIK, and LEI; Wikipedia article titles are not exclusive. Companies merged away by a reviewed merge never match by name.
- Company profile facts, Chinese names, Company ↔ Subject links, and related news are never manufactured: Chinese names come only from an official page that literally contains them, a registered legal name, or a deterministically linked Subject title; nothing is machine-translated. Company merges are explicit and reviewed (`company-merge` with a reason), never automatic.
- Honour publisher API policies (for example Wikimedia's User-Agent contact requirement) through configuration; never fake a browser User-Agent, switch or imitate HTTP clients to pass bot detection, or bypass a policy. Personal contact details stay in the local `.env` and are never committed; each deployment uses its own maintainer contact.
- Maintainer contacts are scoped per publisher, both by client opt-in and by request host (re-checked on every request, including redirects): `SOURCE_CONTACT` (`HTTPClient(..., contact=...)`, rankings with `requires_contact`) reaches only Wikimedia hosts (`wikipedia.org`, `wikimedia.org`, `wikidata.org`); `SEC_CONTACT` (`HTTPClient(..., sec_contact=...)`, profile sources with `requires_sec_contact`) reaches only SEC hosts (`sec.gov`, `data.sec.gov`) in SEC's declared format `IndustryIntelligenceRankingPlatform/0.1 <contact>`. The two are never reused for each other or sent to any other host. Blank and `example.com`/`.invalid`/`.test` placeholders count as unset. Real values exist only in the gitignored `.env`; `.env.example` keeps `your-email@example.com`.
- SEC EDGAR access stays low-frequency and bounded: at most about 2 requests/second within one adapter (0.5 s spacing), 1 s between companies, far below SEC's 10 requests/second ceiling.
- AI is an enhancement layer, never the canonical truth layer (Phase 7). AI output never directly writes ContentSubjectLink, EventContentLink, EventSubjectLink, EventCompanyLink, CompanySubjectLink, identities, aliases, Subject/Company records, rankings, Event membership, or redirects; a suggestion reaches canonical data only through an explicit reviewed CLI action that calls an existing service with its safeguards. AI never creates Subjects or Companies, never chooses database IDs (candidates come from deterministic exact lookup), and never merges automatically.
- No page request calls an AI provider; AI runs only in bounded CLI batches and the UI reads persisted, validated artifacts. AI output never feeds Trending, Search, Rankings, company profiles, or any score.
- AI output is source-grounded: the provider sees only the entity's persisted evidence, every claim cites evidence of that entity, and validation rejects advice, price targets, predictions, multi-source claims for single-source Events, figures absent from the evidence, and wrong-locale text. Displayed AI text is labelled AI-generated with its sources, and the source timeline is always kept.
- AI artifacts are reproducible (provider, served model, prompt version, input hash, evidence IDs); a prompt change is a new prompt version; changed evidence makes artifacts stale. Automated tests never call a real provider. API keys live only in the local gitignored `.env`.
- **AI LANGUAGE POLICY (AI-LP1, 2026-09-30).** Language has three separate layers that must not be bound together again: (1) UI language (en / zh-CN) controls navigation, labels, descriptions, methodology copy, and empty states; (2) Search and entity language stays multilingual (aliases, cross-language canonical resolution, `normalize_subject_name`, Search are unchanged: 微软 → Microsoft, 英伟达 → NVIDIA, 比特币 → Bitcoin); (3) content and AI language is the source's: source titles and evidence stay in their original language, and Event AI is generated only in the Event's primary locale — the one locale every report is written in (`ai_evidence.primary_locale`, from persisted `ContentItem.language`). A Chinese Event gets Chinese Summary / Why It Matters, an English Event English ones: at most two AI tasks (and provider requests) per Event. Cross-language AI generation is discontinued: nothing is translated, a request for a non-primary locale is refused before any provider request or write, and a mixed-language Event (no primary locale) gets no AI. The UI locale never determines the AI content locale, and no page request generates or translates anything. Historical cross-language artifacts (`event_summary_xlang_v1`/`v2`, `event_why_it_matters_xlang_v1`) are retained for audit and reproducibility, never deleted, never served, never used as a fallback, and never required for coverage: an Event's AI is complete when its two primary insights are available.
- Company profile facts come only from adopted sources through reviewed targets; a fetch failure, robots disallow, or HTTP 403 is recorded and never retried with another client or worked around.
- **INTELLIGENCE UPDATE OPERATIONS POLICY (Operations-2, 2026-09-30).** `intelligence-update` is restartable and idempotent (replays by run ID, identity, snapshot key, and input hash; Events only created or joined). Concurrent update runs are forbidden: a real run holds a PostgreSQL session-level advisory lock (`pg_try_advisory_lock`, two-int4 key `update_operations.UPDATE_LOCK_KEY`, dedicated connection) for its whole duration and a second run exits `4` (`already_running`) immediately with no source request, provider request, or write; it never waits or queues; the lock is released explicitly and by PostgreSQL when the process or connection dies; `--plan` never takes it. Source failures remain isolated per source. Source health is derived on read from recorded attempts (`source_attempts`: facts only; no stored current-health table) and the current automatic registry; HTTP success is not ingestion success, and zero new items is not a failure. A `running` run row becomes `interrupted` only when a later process holds the lock, never because of elapsed time. There is no once-per-day rule: intentional reruns on the same day are valid, only simultaneous runs are forbidden. Repair commands, reviewed merges, `event-rebuild`, profile ingestion, embeddings, and Alembic migrations are never part of automatic daily execution. AI remains optional (unattended runs use `--skip-ai` until the owner decides; with `--ai-limit N` at most 2 × N primary-locale requests), and cross-language AI generation remains disabled. An external scheduler must invoke exactly one safe command, `intelligence-update --skip-ai`, and read its exit code: 0 success, 1 failed, 3 degraded, 4 already running.
- **DAILY UPDATE POLICY (Scheduler-1, 2026-09-30).** The daily update runs at 10:00 Australia/Sydney (moved from 08:00 on 2026-09-30; launchd `StartCalendarInterval` in the system time zone, which must stay Australia/Sydney; daylight saving is followed and no fixed UTC offset is encoded; the wrapper refuses to run in any other system zone). The scheduler invokes `intelligence-update` only (`--skip-ai`), through `ops/scheduler/daily_update.py`, never individual ingestion commands. Scheduled AI is disabled (no `--ai-limit`). Migrations are never automatic. Repair commands are never automatic. PostgreSQL advisory locking remains authoritative: there is no scheduler-level lock and no once-per-day rule, so manual reruns stay valid and a simultaneous invocation exits 4. Source health must be checked after the first run and after operational incidents (`source-health`, plus the scheduler log for failures before a run is recorded). The scheduler is disabled (`launchctl bootout`) while an error-level `data-quality-audit` finding or a failed run is unexplained.
- **PRODUCTION DEPLOYMENT POLICY (Deployment-2A, 2026-09-30).** Production is one Linux host with Docker Compose (`deploy/docker-compose.production.yml`, always through `deploy/bin/ii-compose`): only Caddy publishes ports (80/443); PostgreSQL has no published port and sits on an internal network; the backend image serves the API and runs every CLI job (`ii-compose run --rm -T backend …`). Migrations are one explicit operation (`alembic upgrade head` in a one-shot container), never on container start; the order is backup → migration → `data-quality-audit` → start. Real configuration lives only in the root-only server file `/etc/industry-intelligence/production.env`; the repository holds placeholders (`deploy/production.env.example`) and never real env files, hooks, dumps, or TLS material. `NEXT_PUBLIC_API_BASE_URL` and `CORS_ORIGINS` are both exactly `https://<APP_DOMAIN>` (same origin; never `*`). The production daily job is the systemd timer `OnCalendar=*-*-* 10:00:00 Australia/Sydney` running exactly `intelligence-update --skip-ai`; no second lock; exit codes are preserved (no `SuccessExitStatus`) and every non-zero exit notifies through `OnFailure=`. Backups at 11:00 (14 daily + 6 monthly + off-host hook), freshness at 12:30 (`update-freshness`: only `success` within 26 h counts). Provisioning, DNS, and cut-over (Deployment-2B, `docs/cutover.md`) are supervised owner steps; the Mac launchd agent stays enabled until the owner decides after the first scheduled production run.
- **LOCAL RUNTIME POLICY (Local-1, 2026-09-30).** Local daily operation uses native PostgreSQL 16 (launchd agent `local.industry-intelligence.postgresql`, `127.0.0.1:5433`, loopback and Unix socket only, never a LAN or public address) and does not require Docker Desktop. launchd runs `intelligence-update --skip-ai` at 10:00 Australia/Sydney against the database named by the root `.env` `DATABASE_URL`; database credentials live only in that gitignored `.env` (never in a plist, template, or tracked file). The Docker production artifacts (Dockerfiles, `deploy/docker-compose.production.yml`, Caddy, systemd units, backup/notify scripts, restore and cut-over runbooks) remain supported and preserved, and Docker Compose remains the planned production deployment mechanism. Local and production database runtime choices are intentionally different (native macOS server vs the `pgvector/pgvector:pg16` image), including collation (see Known Issues). Exactly one scheduler writes to exactly one local database: after the switch the native database is active and the stopped Docker database (`app_postgres_data`) is rollback-only; it is never deleted before several successful native scheduled runs and an explicit owner decision. Local backups are logical `pg_dump -Fc` before every migration, repair `--apply`, or reviewed merge and otherwise about weekly by hand (README "Local runtime"); there is no local backup timer.

## 6. Core Data Model

- `Source`: external provider, domain, primary language, acquisition type, and base URL.
- `RankList`: a concrete source-published ranking.
- `IngestionBatch`: idempotent snapshot-ingestion record and counts.
- `RankObservation`: append-only subject appearance with rank, title, URL, metric, metadata, and observation time.
- `Subject`: canonical cross-source entity.
- `SubjectAlias`: localized or source-observed names with provenance.
- `SubjectExternalIdentity`: stable source IDs, canonical IDs, tickers, or URLs.
- `CompositeRankingRun`: immutable successful composite calculation keyed by input/config hashes.
- `CompositeRankingEntry`: ranked canonical subject, score, trend, and source breakdown.
- `SearchDocument`: one bounded searchable projection per canonical subject.
- `SubjectEmbedding`: optional current embedding with model, dimension, content hash, and timestamp.
- `ContentItem`: source-neutral news/feed history, stable source identity, URL, publication/observation timestamps, language, excerpt, metadata, and content hash.
- `ContentSubjectLink`: many-to-many deterministic related-news links with strategy and confidence.
- `RankList.list_type`: `ranking` or `market_pulse`; general content uses `news` or `feed`.
- `Company`: minimal cross-domain identity (`canonical_name`, `normalized_name`; `canonical_name` is not unique). The Phase 0 `domain` column was removed because a company can appear in several domains.
- `CompanyAlias`: published names with provenance: `alias_source` is the ranking key or profile source key; `alias_type` is `source_name`, `legal_name`, `transliterated_legal_name`, or `localized_name`; `locale` as published.
- `CompanyIdentity`: unique `(scheme, value)` such as `companiesmarketcap:symbol`, `wikipedia:en` (article title, section fragment kept), `sec:cik`, or `gleif:lei`.
- `Ranking`: one external ranking definition with a stable `key`, `source_key`, domain, publisher, source-provided name, metric type/label/unit, frequency, acquisition, language, and source URL.
- `RankingSnapshot`: one immutable observation, unique `(ranking_id, snapshot_key)`, with `observed_at`, optional `published_at`, `effective_at` (history order), source URL, content hash, status, stored and source entry counts, and metadata. The Phase 0 per-company placeholder table was rebuilt.
- `RankingEntry`: company position in a snapshot with rank, raw name, raw value, optional parsed metric value, resolution strategy, and metadata; unique `(snapshot_id, company_id)`.
- Phase 5 (migration `20260927_08`) removed the unused, all-NULL Phase 0 `companies.website` and `companies.description` columns (the upgrade refuses if any value exists) and added:
- `CompanyProfile` (`company_profiles`, PK/FK `company_id` → `companies` ON DELETE CASCADE): the current field-level merge of a company's source records — `legal_name`, `legal_name_locale`, `website`, `country` (ISO alpha-2, indexed), `headquarters`, `jurisdiction`, `industry`, `categories` (JSON list), `description`, `description_language`, `business_areas` (JSON list), `key_products` (JSON list), `field_sources` (JSON field → source key), `profile_updated_at`. Derived only; rebuilt from `company_profile_sources`.

No page bodies are stored.

- Operations-2 (migration `20260930_12`, revises `20260928_11`; operational history only, no existing table touched) added:
- `UpdateRun` (`update_runs`): one row per real `intelligence-update` invocation, written only under the update lock: UUID `id`, `run_id` (indexed, not unique: a retry with the same `--run-id` is a new invocation), `status` (`running`, `success`, `degraded`, `failed`, `interrupted`; CHECK), `started_at`, `finished_at` (null while running and for interrupted rows), `exit_code`, JSON `options` (sources, skip_ai, ai_limit), JSON `stage_summary` (per stage name, status, reason, duration, failed sources; deltas; never logs or tracebacks), `interrupted_by` (self FK, SET NULL: the run whose lock acquisition proved the row abandoned). ### Events (Phase 6)

- Input is persisted `ContentItem` rows only; Event building never fetches. `event-build [--since ISO8601-with-offset] [--limit N] [--dry-run]` processes content items without an `EventContentLink` in publication order (`coalesce(published_at, observed_at)`, then ID) inside one transaction (advisory lock `events`); `--dry-run` runs the same code and rolls back, printing multi-content clusters. Re-running creates nothing (processed items are skipped). Since Phase 6.1, `event-rebuild --from --to [--dry-run]` re-runs the current rules over a publication window (see Phase 6.1 checkpoint E).
- Normalization (`event_resolution.py`): `extract_headline` keeps the leading `【headline】` of feed titles; `strip_boilerplate` removes only `市场消息/快讯/突发：` and a leading `N连板`; `normalize_title` applies NFKC, casefold, punctuation/symbol → space (decimal points inside numbers and thousands separators handled), collapsed whitespace. Features: CJK character bigrams plus Latin/digit word tokens without a small English stopword list; similarity = Dice coefficient (Python, locale-independent; pg_trgm is not used because its CJK behaviour depends on the database locale).
- Candidates: members of existing Events in the same domain whose content is published within `TIME_WINDOW` = 24 h of the item (live true pairs were 0.01–0.44 h apart).
- Pair rules, in order: different domain, outside window → no match. Conflicts (reject): both have a specific type and they differ; both have linked Subjects (or Companies) and share none; both have a CJK attribution prefix and they differ; numeric conflict (neither headline's numbers all appear in the other item's title+description). Then evidence: `exact_headline` (identical normalized headline, ≥ 8 non-space characters; allowed within one source); otherwise items from the same source never merge; `attribution_headline` (identical CJK prefix before `：`, 2–24 chars, and statement Dice ≥ 0.6); `shared_entity_headline` (shared canonical Subject or Company from existing deterministic links, and headline Dice ≥ 0.7). Anything else is `no_identity_evidence`: title similarity or time alone never merges.
- Event rule: an item joins an Event only if it matches at least one member and conflicts with none of the members in the window; if it matches more than one Event it seeds a new Event (`ambiguous`, candidate IDs recorded). Events are never merged automatically.
- Event type: keyword families (English and Chinese) on the normalized headline; a type is assigned only when exactly one family matches, else `general`; `game_release` only in `gaming`. An Event's type is its members' single specific type, else `general`.
- Canonical title: the headline (bracketed feed headline where present) of the member ordered by: a headline without feed boilerplate first, then earliest publication time, then source name, then content ID; kept verbatim (not translated, not rewritten). A later report changes it only if every earlier headline carries boilerplate and the later one does not.
- Diagnostics: every pair decision records the title similarity and time delta; seeds keep the nearest rejected candidate (highest similarity) with its rejection reason, for auditing missed merges.
- Entities: `EventSubjectLink` from the members' `ContentSubjectLink` rows (merged subjects resolve through `subject_redirects`); `EventCompanyLink` from those subjects' `CompanySubjectLink` rows (company redirects resolve). No independent name, alias, fuzzy, embedding, or LLM matching. `event-relink` rebuilds all derived entity links; run it after `content-relink` or `company-subject-link`.
- Reviewed merge (`event-merge CANONICAL DUPLICATE --reason`): follows redirects; moves content links, records `event_redirects`, recomputes both Events and their entity links; refuses a missing reason, self/cycle, repeat merges, different domains, two different specific types, disjoint Subject or Company links, and publication ranges more than 7 days apart.
- Read model/API: `GET /api/v1/events` (`domain`, `subject_id` (redirect-resolved), `company_id` (redirect-resolved), `event_type`, `from`/`to` (timezone-aware; overlap with the Event's publication range), `limit` 20 default / 100 max, `offset`; recent first by `last_published_at`; redirected and empty Events excluded; items carry up to 4 sources, languages, and up to 3 Subject/Company previews with totals); `GET /api/v1/events/{id}` (redirect-aware with `requested_id`/`redirected`; entities, coverage by source and language, time block with `basis: content_publication`, 5-item content preview; 404 unknown, 422 malformed); `GET /api/v1/events/{id}/content` (`limit` 10 default / 50 max, `offset`, `order` asc|desc; original URLs, source, language, publication time, link strategy/confidence). Page loads read persisted data only.
